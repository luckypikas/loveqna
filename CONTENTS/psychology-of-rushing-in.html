The Psychology of Rushing In: The Danger of Skipping the Vetting Phase

• A LoveQnA Forensic Narrative • Updated: August 2026
Speed is not a proxy for depth.
Intensity is not a proxy for intimacy.
When you rush into a relationship,
you aren't falling for the person.
You are falling for their potential.
And potential is just a projection of your own desires.

One of the most dangerous dynamics in modern dating is the accelerated relationship. It feels like a movie. You meet someone, the chemistry is blinding, the late-night text threads are endless, and within a matter of weeks, you are discussing moving in together, meeting families, and mapping out a shared future.

Society romanticizes this. We call it "love at first sight" or "when you know, you know." But in the realm of Behavioral Relationship Forensics, we call it something else: Skipping the Vetting Phase.

I. The Illusion of Accelerated Intimacy

Intimacy takes time. It is built on a foundation of observed consistency, shared adversity, and conflict resolution. When a relationship moves at warp speed, it creates a psychological illusion of intimacy. You feel like you know them deeply because you’ve shared trauma, secrets, and extreme affection in a short window.

But sharing secrets is easy. Sustaining character is hard. When you rush, you are not interacting with the real person. You are interacting with their Representative—the highly curated, best-behavior version of themselves they send to the front lines of early dating. The Representative never gets tired, never gets angry, and always says the right thing. But the Representative cannot sustain the act forever.

II. Eagerness vs. Love-Bombing

How do you tell the difference between someone who is genuinely excited about you, and someone who is manipulating you through speed? Here is the clinical breakdown:

  • Genuine Eagerness: They want to see you, but they respect your time. They show consistent, warm interest. If you ask to slow down, they agree and adjust their pace without guilt-tripping you. Their words match their actions over a sustained period.
  • Love-Bombing: They manufacture destiny. They use phrases like "I've never felt this way before" within days of meeting you. They overwhelm you with grand gestures, gifts, and demands on your time. If you ask to slow down, they become injured, cold, or try to convince you that your boundaries are "ruining the connection."
"Love-bombing isn't about loving you. It's about securing you quickly before you have time to see the cracks in the foundation."

III. Why We Hit the Gas (The Drivers)

It takes two to rush. Often, we ignore the speed because the dopamine rush of being deeply desired masks our own psychological vulnerabilities. People hit the gas for three primary reasons:

  1. The Scarcity Mindset: The fear that if you don't lock this person down immediately, someone else will, or they will lose interest.
  2. Emotional Novocaine: Using the intense high of a new relationship to numb the pain of past trauma, loneliness, or a recent breakup (The Rebound Effect).
  3. The Fantasy Fix: Falling in love with the idea of being in a relationship, rather than doing the actual, tedious work of evaluating the person standing in front of you.

IV. The Vetting Phase: What You Miss When You Sprint

The Vetting Phase is the first 90 to 120 days of dating. It is a critical period of observation where you gather data on how a person navigates the world. When you rush, you willingly blindfold yourself to vital information. By skipping the Vetting Phase, you miss out on observing:

  • Stress Responses: How do they act when a flight is canceled, when they get bad news, or when a waiter makes a mistake?
  • Conflict Resolution: What happens when you tell them "no"? Do they pout, stonewall, or communicate like an adult?
  • Consistency: Do their Tuesday moods match their Friday moods?
  • Financial Habits: Do they live within their means, or are they hiding massive, impulsive debt?

V. The Collapse: When the Representative Clocks Out

Eventually, the adrenaline wears off. The dopamine levels stabilize. The exhaustion of playing the perfect partner sets in, and the Representative clocks out.

This is when the real person steps forward. If you rushed in, this moment feels like a terrifying bait-and-switch. Suddenly, the person who texted you "good morning beautiful" every day at 7:00 AM is now irritable, distant, and dismissive. You will spend the next six months to a year fighting desperately to get "the old them" back.

But here is the brutal truth of Relationship Forensics: The old them never existed. You are finally meeting the person you should have vetted on day one.

VI. The LoveQnA Survival Protocol

Pacing is your ultimate defense mechanism. Slowing a relationship down does not kill the romance; it kills the illusion.

Take a breath. Let the silence breathe. Keep your own hobbies, your own friends, and your own schedule. If a connection is genuine, it will survive the test of time. If someone is rushing you, they are almost always running from something—and trying to drag you along for the ride.
