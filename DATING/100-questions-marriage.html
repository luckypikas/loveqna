100 Questions You Should Always Ask Before Marriage

• Relationship Diagnostics | Lucky Pikas Studios

Love is an emotion. Marriage is a legally binding contract. You are not just choosing a romantic partner; you are choosing your emergency contact, your financial co-signer, and the person who will manage your health decisions.

Most divorces do not happen because of a sudden loss of love. They happen because of a slow, devastating realization of incompatibility in money, family boundaries, and household labor. Before you sign the contract, you must perform absolute due diligence.

Part 1: The Financial Audit (Money & Debt)

  1. What is your exact current credit score?
  2. How much debt (student loans, credit cards, taxes) are you bringing into this marriage?
  3. Do we merge all of our bank accounts, or maintain separate accounts and a joint bill account?
  4. What is the maximum amount of money either of us can spend without consulting the other?
  5. Do you believe in taking out loans for things other than a house or a car?
  6. If my family or your family asks for a loan, how do we handle it?
  7. How much should we save for retirement each month?
  8. Are we signing a prenuptial agreement to protect pre-marital assets?
  9. If one of us loses our job, what is the immediate backup plan?
  10. Who is physically paying the bills each month?
  11. What is your definition of "financial ruin"?
  12. Do you believe in tithing or donating a percentage of our income?
  13. Do you expect me to financially support your parents when they get older?
  14. What is your biggest financial regret so far?
  15. If we win the lottery, what is the very first thing we do with the money?

Part 2: Family Boundaries & In-Laws

  1. How much influence do your parents have over your decisions?
  2. If your mother/father disrespects me, will you defend me publicly?
  3. How often do you expect us to visit your family?
  4. Will our parents have a key to our home?
  5. If we argue, do you discuss the details with your family or friends?
  6. Are we expected to spend every major holiday with your family?
  7. Do you have any family members who are not allowed in our home?
  8. How will we handle it if our parents disagree with how we raise our kids?
  9. Are we expected to take in elderly parents to live with us in the future?
  10. What traditions from your childhood do you want to keep, and which do you want to abandon?

Part 3: Conflict & Crisis Management

  1. When you get overwhelmingly angry, do you need space or do you need to talk immediately?
  2. Have you ever used the "silent treatment" as a form of punishment?
  3. What is an absolute, non-negotiable dealbreaker that would end this marriage instantly?
  4. If we face a severe crisis, do you want to seek professional counseling or therapy?
  5. Is there anything from your past that could suddenly appear and affect our marriage?
  6. Do you believe that threatening divorce during an argument is ever acceptable?
  7. How do you handle apologies? (Do you require them, and can you give them?)
  8. What is the most effective way for me to comfort you when you fail?
  9. Have you fully forgiven the people who hurt you in your past?
  10. If I tell you that something you did hurt my feelings, what is your first instinct?

Part 4: Children & Parenting

  1. Do you absolutely, 100% want children, and how many?
  2. What happens if we find out we are biologically unable to have children?
  3. If we have children, who is staying home, or will we use daycare?
  4. What are your exact views on physical discipline (spanking) for children?
  5. What religion or moral philosophy will our children be raised in?
  6. How will we handle it if our child comes out as LGBTQ+?
  7. How will we fund their education?
  8. Do you believe in strictly monitoring a teenager's internet and phone access?
